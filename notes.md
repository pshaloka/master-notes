# The Realm 

- The River: The Queenswend flows into the Kingscourse at the Marshmeet, and then on into the wide sea. 
    - patrolled by long, flat-bottomed swan boats sent out by the Horn Knight 
    - the shallows are pleasant, but dragon-eels have been known to hunt the shallows 
    - few fords and no bridges south of the Marshmeet, and no way to cross save for catching a fishing boat at the Weeping Town

- Gray Open Triangles (Soft Crags)
    - chalky white cliffs 
    - weeping in the heavy rain, rivulets of milky tears running down the cliff face and staining the sea - screeching gulls, white on white
    - would be easy enough to cut handholds in, but it would be slow-going with your steeds - switchback path of loose and crumbly chalk 
- Golden Dunes (Dry Hills)
    - sandy dunes, loose vegetation desperately clinging for purchase
    - small, biting flies, the smell of salt and a faint stench of rot 
    - traveling caravan, light wagons of driftwood on sleds pulled by lean and hungry looking dogs, laden with bells
- Crimson Grass (Vivid Heath)
    - see Devilweed entry 
    - crimson, magenta, scarlet, fleshy-pink: you never knew there were so many shades of red in the world
    - an aurochs lying in a daze, tongue lolling, breathing slow, pelt covered in burrs
    - the heady perfume of a winery, worked by beautiful youths in various states of undress, working a wine press
- Pale Green Tree and Grass (Soft Bog) - Wormrot Swamp
    - marshy, sandy wetland: mud and sand oozeing and squelching with every step 
    - fat, languid butterflies the size of dinner plates seem to float like kites in the warm, still air 
    - the ground is carpeted with springy moss, difficult to tell where the ground ends and the water begins 
    - wooden town on stilts - food is laid out, market stalls display goods, and not a single soul is to be seen 
- Pale Green Swamp (Teaming Bog) - Codger's Bog or the Middlemire 
    - Large lizards basking in the sparse patches of sun, slinking quietly into the water on your approach 
    - Scuttling, chitinous bugs, chittering amidst the trees 
    - Hanging, Spanish moss rustling as a wild herd of boarse move through the undergrowth, trailed some distance by an old, almost lame wolf
- Gold Solid Triangle (Sparse Peaks)
    - The King's Crown or The Jester's Teeth: large veins of pyrite streak the mountain side
    - the golden light, flashing uncomfortably bright in the noon-day sun
    - a desperate vulture circling over dead slopes where nothing grows or moves
    - a dejected group of miners with a dwarvish look about them, they seem desperate, ill-at ease, and fingering their pick-axes
- Green Grass (Still Plain)
    - see Willowgrass entry
    - tall, pale green grass like the fronds of the willow tree sway gently in the breeze
    - almost as though someone is murmmering something you jut can't quite make-out 
    - you see the tracks and scat of what looks like a largeish feline
    - dog-sized grasshoppers bound out in front of you

  # Flora and Fauna

1. Horse - It's just a horse, but it's yours. 
2. Hindish - Delicate, deer-like steed. Nimble, deft, and impossibly fast. Frail and flighty. Dappled, tawny coat. Not truly suited for combat or long-distance travel, but unmatched in swiftness.
3. Boarse - Not the fastest and tires easily. Has a brute and wily strength, which doubles when injured. Wild-tempered, and difficult to control, but fiercely loyal. Tastes delicious.
4. Horx - Stolid, dependable, huge. Not winning any beauty contests or races. Long horns can be inconvenient in tight spaces, and must be fed much more than other mounts. Can shoulder almost any burden. 
5. Mottled Bear - Slow in mind and body. Hidden strength, but a gentle spirit. Picky eaters. People love them, but leaves something to be desired as a true mount. 
6. Ram -  Huge, twisting horns. Can scale all but the steepest of slopes with ease. Loves the thrill of combat, but tends to get a bit carried away. Hard-headed at the best of times.
7. Unicorn - Thick, gray hide, raw power, surprising speed. Requires a ridiculous amount of food and pampering. Difficult to train, impossible to truly tame, but will be a steadfast friend to a worthy companion. Gets lonely without another animal to keep it company.
8. Elephant Beetle - Tricky to maintain a proper seat in combat. Offputting smell. Quick in short bursts. Razor sharp mandibles. Just wants to be loved, and is eager to please.
9. Pegasus - A winged horse. Can't gain altitude, but it can glide. Refuses to touch water. Only drinks wine, and is a mean drunk. 
10. Wingless Wyrm - Large, salamander like creature with slightly moist skin. Only eats fresh meat. Once a day, can belch forth fire and smoke at your command. Languid, and difficult to motivate. Hates Boarse.
11. Palebeast - A sickly, queer-looking mount with black, sightless eyes. Unnaturally strong and durable. Sensitive skin must be covered in direct sunlight, and unnerves most common-folk. Prone to periods of listless melancholy.
12. Hartish - Very rare, coats seem to be perpetually dappled with shadow, as though half-standing beneath the boughs of some great tree. The perfect combination of speed and strength for a knight, but these noble creatures will never back down or listen to what it deems to be craven commands.

Devilweed: Acrid, bitter smelling, voracious. Can't cross running water. Male plants have small black burrs, female plants emit an intoxicating scent. Requires assistance to reproduce. One disgraced Seer foresaw that devilweed would cover the world.

Willowgrass: Tall, delicate fronds that sway in the slightest breeze. Overs some nutrition, but hardly worth the effort to harvest. Needs to be burned. Can be brewed into a delicious, delicate beer that makes the drinker melancholy the next day, though the drinking is sweet.

Blushbough: Soft, almost rubbery trees with long, fan shaped pink leaves. The bark can be used in poltucies and tinctures that will cure common ailments, but leave the user feeling bitter and resentful
